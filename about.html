Walking the Tree of Candidates

How the page follows sinh-Gordon spectral data at a fixed internal τ, through the openings of nodes, to three candidates for constrained Willmore tori, and how it draws them in ℝ⁴.

1The idea in brief

A constant mean curvature torus has a spectral curve $\Sigma:\ \nu^2=\lambda a(\lambda)$, and the same curves describe a family of constrained Willmore tori in the 4-sphere (Bohle). The page fixes a conformal class, the internal $\tau$, and moves through these curves along a tree. Each edge of the tree is a deformation of the curve within one genus that keeps $\tau$ fixed. A branching happens when two points of the curve that the immersion cannot tell apart, a resonance pair, meet over the unit circle: there a double point is inserted and opened, and the genus goes up by one.

At such an event the curve carries enough points with trivial monodromy to close a torus. These are the candidates. Candidate 1 is found on the genus 1 edge, and candidates 2 and 3 on the genus 2 edge; they are drawn with the generalized Sym formula, which needs two such points and gives a torus in ℝ⁴.

2Spectral data and resonance pairs

The curve is $\nu^2=\lambda a(\lambda)$ with $a(\lambda)=-\prod_j(\lambda-\alpha_j)(1-\bar\alpha_j\lambda)$, branched at $0$, $\infty$, the points $\alpha_j$ in the unit disc and their reflections. The differentials $\Theta$ with double poles over $0$ and $\infty$ and imaginary periods give a map $$q=(q^1,q^2):\ \Sigma\to\mathbb C^2/\Gamma,$$ the logarithms of the multipliers of the frame in the two directions of the plane. The lattice $\Gamma=2\pi i\Lambda^*$ is dual to the lattice $\Lambda$ of the torus, and its conformal class is the internal $\tau$.

A resonance pair is a pair of points $P\neq Q$ with $q(P)-q(Q)\in\Gamma$. In genus 0 there is one pair for each lattice vector. The page marks the pairs of the three shortest vectors, one from each nonzero class of $\Gamma/2\Gamma$, and follows them along the tree.

The internal $\tau$ is a point of the domain $F_6$: the three classes are labelled 1 = [1], 2 = [τ], 3 = [1 + τ], and the six copies of $F_1$ in the pad are labelled by the order of their shortest vectors by length. The shortest class is opened first.

3The generalized Sym formula

Let $F(z,\lambda)$ be the frame, with $F(0,\lambda)=I$, and let $u_j$ be an eigenvector of the polynomial Killing field at a point $\lambda_j$ of the curve. The rows $\psi_j=u_j^{T}F(z,\lambda_j)$ are holomorphic sections of one quaternionic line bundle, and $$f=Q(\psi_1)\,Q(\psi_2)^{-1},\qquad Q(a,b)=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\-\bar b&\bar a\end{pmatrix},$$ is a conformal immersion into $\mathbb H=\mathbb R^4$ for any two points (Bohle–Leschke–Pedit–Pinkall; Bohle). For $\lambda_1,\lambda_2$ on the unit circle it is the Sym–Bobenko formula of a CMC surface in $S^3$.

It closes on the torus when both points have trivial multipliers ±1. A branch point always has multiplier ±1, and so does a resonance pair at the moment it arrives over the unit circle as a double point (a σ-node). The signs form a character of the lattice. When the two characters differ, the torus closes on an index 2 sublattice, and the page draws that double cover. Its conformal class is the external $\tau$.

4The tree

  1. Genus 0. The curve $\nu^2=-\lambda$ has no parameters; only the scale of $\Lambda$ changes. Coming from the vacuum, the pair of the shortest class arrives first over the unit circle, as a σ-node at $\lambda=-1$.
  2. The opening. The node is replaced by a pair of branch points $\alpha=-r$, $1/\bar\alpha$, with $r$ just below 1.
  3. The genus 1 edge. $r$ decreases, with the scale of the lattice adjusted so that $\tau$ stays fixed, while the other two marked pairs are followed. The first of them to arrive over the unit circle gives candidate 1: the branch point $\alpha$ and the new node as Sym points.
  4. The genus 2 edge. The new node is opened too, and the edge is followed with the period ratio fixed, parametrized by the scale $t=\sqrt{|\alpha_1\alpha_2|}$ of the branch points. When the remaining pair arrives, the curve has three points with trivial multipliers: $\alpha_1$, $\alpha_2$ and the node. Candidate 2 takes $\alpha_1,\alpha_2$, candidate 3 takes $\alpha_1$ and the node. All candidates on one curve share it and its Willmore energy, and differ only in which points are the Sym points.
  5. Not opening. The tree also branches the other way at candidate 1: the node is not opened, and the genus 1 edge continues, with the arrived pair now two points over the unit circle. At the next σ-node (the other pair arriving, or the arrived pair leaving the circle again) $\alpha$ and the node give candidate 1′. Since the conformal class of a candidate depends only on the classes of its two Sym points, candidates 1 and 2 have the same external $\tau$, and so do candidates 3 and 1′ when the other pair arrives.

Along every path tried so far the Willmore energy increases from the vacuum to the last candidate.

The tree view plots $W$ (on the closing lattice) against the path: the opening on the left axis, the genus 1 edge on the first third, the genus 2 edge on the rest. Drag the dot, or use the arrow keys (up and down switch to the dashed branch to candidate 1′); the surface, the curve in the λ-plane and the note follow it. Three candidates side by side shows candidates 1, 2 and 3 in three panes, and the map under the τ pad shows where in $F_6$ candidates 2 and 3 exist. Dragging a branch point in genus 2 moves to the curve's own $\tau$ and scale, and recomputes the tree.

5Before the candidates

The formula makes sense at every point of the tree, with the Sym points taken from the pairs that will become the candidate. Since $u_j$ is also an eigenvector of the monodromy, $\psi_j(z+w)=\mu_j(w)\psi_j(z)$, and so $$f(z+w)=\mu_1(w)\,f(z)\,\mu_2(w)^{-1}.$$ At a marked pair off the unit circle the multipliers are real, so the lattice acts by homotheties of $\mathbb R^4$ about $0$: the surface is a plane, which closes up exactly when the pair arrives.

The note under the tree gives the factors $c_k$ with $f(z+e_k)=c_k f(z)$ on the drawn cell; they are 1 at the candidates.

6The open ends

For some $\tau$ the genus 2 edge reaches the open end of the space of genus 2 curves before the remaining pair arrives: both branch points go to $0$. In the coordinate $\lambda/t$ the curve tends to an elliptic curve whose $\tau$ is the internal one. Candidate 2 tends to the quotient of the torus by $z\mapsto -z$, a round sphere covered twice and branched at the four half-periods, with $W\to8\pi$. Numerically the distance from a round sphere decreases like $t^{1/2}$.

Moving $\tau$ towards this region, the arrival slides down to $t=0$, and candidates 2 and 3 merge continuously into the open end.

Near an open end, spikes mark the four branch points of the limit. Check the limit shape measures, after a Möbius normalization in $S^4$, how close the surface is to the doubly covered sphere.

7Walls of F₆

Where the two classes after the opened one are equally long (for example $\operatorname{Re}\tau=\tfrac12$), both pairs arrive together on the genus 1 edge. Then all three candidates lie on the genus 1 curve, which has three points with trivial multipliers: $\alpha$ and two nodes. Candidate 2 coincides with candidate 1, and the third choice, the two nodes, is a CMC torus in $S^3$ with a rectangular external $\tau$. Across such a wall the candidates continue; only the labels of the classes change.

8From ℝ⁴ to the screen

A torus with a branch point among its Sym points lies on no round 3-sphere, so it has to be projected. The automatic projection uses stereographic projection when the surface does lie on a 3-sphere. Otherwise it reads the surface in $S^4$, moves it by a Möbius transformation so that the mean of its points is the centre, and projects stereographically from the direction in which it is thinnest. A generic projection of a surface in $\mathbb R^4$ has self-intersections ending in cross-caps, across which one sees the other side.

The projection is chosen under Appearance. The texture fourth coordinate colours the surface by the coordinate the projection drops, and the 4D turn sliders rotate the surface in ℝ⁴ before projecting.
